    A weekend at ours

    Not a feature list. A weekend.

    ToHuus Hub shows up in small moments, the ones where you'd otherwise shout up the stairs. Here's what that looks like on an ordinary Saturday and Sunday.

    Saturday
    09:30Coffee, notebook

    "What do we eat this week?"

    The usual Saturday question. ToHuus Hub fills the empty evenings with recipes that fit what's already in the house: what has to go first, what you like, what you haven't had in a while. One more tap puts every missing ingredient on the shopping list.

    On Wednesday friends ask you out? "We're eating out tonight" moves the rest of the week back by a day.

    10:40Waiting for the pickup

    "Can you grab wrapping paper?"

    The REWE order is still being packed, so Lea goes in for the few things it forgot. In store mode the list is sorted by aisle, in the order she usually walks that shop, and it keeps working at the back where the signal gives up.

    At home you type "wrapping paper and 2 milk" into the list and it shows up on her phone a moment later. Anything someone tapped "I'll get it" on carries their name, so nobody buys the cream twice. At the till, "done shopping" books the whole basket into stock.

    Nobody types in the shopping.

    Upload the pickup notice and the eBon instead. ToHuus Hub reads both, matches the order against the receipt and shows what changed: the substitution, the item that wasn't there, the new product that still needs a shelf. You check it once and it's in stock.

    Receipt photos, QR receipts and pasted text work too. Right now the parsers know REWE; other shops are welcome as contributions.

    One look, and you know.

    19:20Friends over

    The TV takes the orders.

    Two taps start a party. The TV shows a QR code and what's on the menu tonight. Guests scan it and order from their own phones, picking from what's actually in the house. The kitchen works through new, in progress and ready, and the TV shows which numbers can be picked up at the counter.

    If two people want the last cola, the first order gets it. When nobody has ordered for a while the party ends by itself, and the recap puts whatever ran out on the shopping list.

    Most evenings it's smaller. Lea on the sofa orders a cappuccino, extra hot, and whoever is in the kitchen gets a push. If you like, the whole app dresses up as "Fredirando", a slightly silly home-delivery service in orange.

    House rules

    What's stuck to our fridge door.

    The rules we built the app around. They're the reason it still gets used after the first week.

    • Half full is an answer.

      Fruit, veg and bread are just full, half, low or empty. Packets and bottles get counted. Coffee from the machine isn't tracked at all.

    • Nobody types in the shopping.

      Stock comes in through receipts, orders, photos and the barcode scanner. Typing is the fallback, not the plan.

    • Nobody keeps it all in their head.

      Built for brains that forget things: few taps, gentle nudges instead of a wall of data, and it still works on a bad day.

    • Guests see names, not numbers.

      Babysitter and guest links show places and the menu. Quantities, the rest of the house and your settings stay private.

    • Opened is a different clock.

      An open jar doesn't keep like a closed one. Opened, frozen and leftover things get their own dates and move up to "eat first".

    • The house has chores too.

      Water filter, smoke detectors, descaling, tyres, passports. They come back on their own, and the spare filter cartridge lands on the list.

    • It stays in the house.

      One SQLite file on your own server. No account with us, no telemetry, no cloud in between.

    The kitchen drawer

    Everything else is in here.

    • RecipesImport from any recipe site, a HelloFresh card or a photo. Thermomix steps keep their time, temperature and speed.
    • SpendingMonthly totals by shop and category, straight from the receipts, plus a hint when something got more expensive.
    • NotificationsWeb push to installed phones, with buttons in the notification. Or ntfy, a webhook, e-mail, Home Assistant.
    • VoiceThe shopping list syncs with a Home Assistant to-do list, so Assist or Siri can add to it.
    • FamilyInvite the others with a link. Everyone picks their own password, nobody shares yours.
    • BackupsNightly snapshots, restore from the app, and an optional copy to WebDAV or Nextcloud.

    How it started

    We built it for our own cellar.

    We're Till and Lea, a family with a small kid in northern Germany. Our house has a fridge, a chest freezer in the cellar, a pantry that's never quite tidy and a utility room where things go to hide.

    For years one of us knew where everything was and what was running out, and the other one asked. That works until it doesn't: the third bottle of ketchup, the parcel tape nobody can find, the "didn't you buy milk?" at six in the evening.

    So we built ToHuus Hub for ourselves, with a lot of help from Claude. The one rule: it has to work on a tired Tuesday, so nobody has to keep it all in their head. Now it runs on a small server at home and everyone in the house uses it.

    It's free and AGPL-licensed. There's no company behind it, no paid tier and nothing to sign up for. If it helps your household too, that makes us happy. Issues and pull requests are welcome.

    Install

    Moved in within a few minutes.

    All you need is Docker, on a NAS at home or a small VPS. No licence key, no account, no cost.

    1. Save the compose fileSet BASE_URL, your name and a password.
    2. Start itdocker compose up -d, then open http://localhost:8080.
    3. Want to look around first?Set SEED_DEMO: "true" before the first start and you get a demo household.
    4. Put it behind HTTPSYour usual reverse proxy is fine. Then add it to your phone's home screen.
    docker-compose.yml
    services:
  tohuus:
    image: ghcr.io/tillheidrich/tohuus-hub:latest
    container_name: tohuus-hub
    restart: unless-stopped
    ports:
      - "8080:8080"
    volumes:
      - ./data:/data
    environment:
      BASE_URL: https://home.example.org
      ADMIN_NAME: Alex
      ADMIN_PASSWORD: change-me
      LANGUAGE: en          # or de
      SEED_DEMO: "false"
      # optional, for photo imports:
      # VISION_BASE_URL: https://api.mistral.ai/v1
    or build from source
    git clone https://github.com/tillheidrich/tohuus-hub.git
cd tohuus-hub
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d --build
