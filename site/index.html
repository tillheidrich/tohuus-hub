Why we built it

Three questions, every single day.

17:45, standing at the fridgeWhat's for dinner?
Parcel half packedWhere is the tape?
Somewhere in aisle fourDo we still have milk?

Less remembering. Fewer arguments.

In most homes, one person carries the household in their head: what's running low, what expires on Thursday, which drawer the plasters live in. That's the mental load, and it is tiring.

ToHuus Hub moves that knowledge out of one head and into a place everyone can see. It was built with ADHD in mind: few taps, nothing to keep track of yourself, and gentle nudges instead of a wall of data.

  • Quick to update
  • Good enough beats exact
  • Shared by everyone at home
What it does

Stock comes in through what you already have.

Most pantry apps die the same way: somebody has to type everything in, nobody does, and the app gets deleted. ToHuus Hub reads receipts, orders and photos instead, and is happy with "half full".

01 · Today

One screen for the whole house.

Open the app and you see what matters right now: what expires soon, what's empty, what's running low, the shopping list, tonight's best recipe, imports waiting for a look and guest orders.

  • Where can I find …? Search sits right at the top.
  • Use something up with one tap, straight from the list.
02 · Stock by room

Fridge, freezer, cellar. At a glance.

Every place in your home gets its own list. Fruit, veg and bread are tracked coarsely as full, half, low or empty. Packets and bottles are counted. Coffee from the machine doesn't need counting at all.

  • Best-before per batch, and the oldest gets used first.
  • Move things between rooms, see history and weekly usage.
  • Barcode scan with the phone camera, product photos from Open Food Facts.
03 · Where is what?

"Left shelf, top compartment."

Give anything an exact spot, and every room a short direction. Then anyone can search for the plasters, the thermometer, the fuse box or the spare keys, including things you would never count.

  • Babysitter link: exactly that search, read-only, with your note on top: emergency numbers, bedtime.
  • Guest links show names and places, never quantities, and expire on their own.
04 · Shopping list

One list, every phone, live.

The list syncs between everyone at home while you shop. Things that run low land on it by themselves. Tick something off and it goes straight back into stock.

  • Grouped by category, with who added what.
  • Share it as plain text when someone else does the run.
05 · Receipts & orders

Upload the receipt. Skip the typing.

ToHuus Hub reads REWE pickup and delivery notices and the eBon, and matches the order against the receipt, so whatever you handed back at pickup doesn't end up in your stock.

  • PDFs, digital receipts behind a QR code, photos and pasted text.
  • A review step for substitutions, missing items, new products and where things go.
  • It learns your shop's cryptic abbreviations, product by product.

The parsers are deterministic and tested. Other shops are welcome as contributions.

06 · Recipes & week plan

Dinner, decided by what's actually there.

Recipes are ranked by stock, expiry dates, favourites and how long ago you last made them. Fill the empty evenings of the week in one go, and put every missing ingredient on the list with one more tap.

  • "We're eating out": tonight's meal and everything after it moves back a day.
  • Import recipes from any site with recipe data, from photos or text. Thermomix steps included.
  • Already plan in Cookidoo? Your week comes over through Home Assistant every three hours.
07 · Guest bar

Guests order from the sofa.

Share a link or a QR code at the barbecue. Guests pick drinks, ice cream and snacks from what is really in stock, with options like oat milk or toppings. You get a push, accept, serve, and the stock is updated.

  • Fredirando: an optional, slightly silly home-delivery theme. Orange, "driver on the way", the works.

Your data stays home

One SQLite file on your own machine. JSON export. No telemetry, no account with us, no cloud dependency. Runs on a NAS or a small VPS in the EU.

  • StorageSQLite
  • Memory~60 MB
  • Trackingnone

Photo reading is optional

Plug in any OpenAI-compatible vision model, for example Mistral in the EU or Ollama on your own hardware. Or leave it out: everything else works without it, and photos can still reach the app through your assistant.

  • Vision modeloptional, swappable
  • ExamplesMistral, Ollama, vLLM

Who it's for

Made for households first.

Main use

Families

Two adults, kids, a dog, a cellar full of things. This is the home it was built in and for: shared lists, a week plan, a babysitter link.

Works well

Install

Up and running in a few minutes.

All you need is Docker, on a NAS at home or a small VPS. No licence key, no account, no cost.

  1. Save the compose fileSet BASE_URL, your name and a password.
  2. Start itdocker compose up -d, then open http://localhost:8080.
  3. Look around first?Set SEED_DEMO: "true" before the first start for a demo household.
  4. Put it behind HTTPSYour usual reverse proxy is fine. Then add it to your phone's home screen.
docker-compose.yml
services:
  tohuus:
    image: ghcr.io/tillheidrich/tohuus-hub:latest
    container_name: tohuus-hub
    restart: unless-stopped
    ports:
      - "8080:8080"
    volumes:
      - ./data:/data
    environment:
      BASE_URL: https://home.example.org
      ADMIN_NAME: Alex
      ADMIN_PASSWORD: change-me
      LANGUAGE: en          # or de
      SEED_DEMO: "false"
      # optional, for photo imports:
      # VISION_BASE_URL: https://api.mistral.ai/v1
or build from source
git clone https://github.com/tillheidrich/tohuus-hub.git
cd tohuus-hub
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d --build
To Huus is Low German for “at home”.

ToHuus Hub was made by Till and Lea, a family near Hamburg, for their own kitchen, cellar and freezer. It started because we were tired of the same three questions, and of the small arguments that come with them.

It is free and licensed under the AGPL. There is no company behind it, no paid tier and nothing to sign up for. If it helps your household too, that makes us happy. Issues and pull requests are welcome.
