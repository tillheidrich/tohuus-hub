Mental load

The questions one person usually carries around.

Usually one person knows what is missing, what needs eating and where the parcel tape lives. In families, in couples and in flat shares alike. ToHuus takes that part over, so you can share it, or stop thinking about it altogether.

Now in ToHuus

01Home, not house

For every kind of home, as it really is.

On first start ToHuus asks who lives with you. That only shapes suggestions. Nothing is locked, and you can switch any time.

  • Just meNo votes and no “who is getting it”, just your lists.
  • The two of usShared lists and votes for dinner, no kids section.
  • FamilyEverything, including kids and guests.
  • Flat shareShared and personal places, no family wording.

Only the places you really have.

ToHuus does not assume a fridge, a cellar or a chest freezer. You name your places yourselves, nest, sort and archive them. Anything without a place shows up under “not assigned to a place yet”. That is fine too.

  • Kitchenette
  • Shared fridge
  • Jonas’ shelf
  • Hallway shelf
  • Chest freezer in the cellar
  • Not assigned to a place yet
02Everyday

One look in the morning is enough.

“Today” shows on one page what comes first, and exactly one next step. The shopping list is current on every phone at once.

  • Today firstAt most four lines from your real data: what needs eating, what has run out, what is on the list. No dashboard tiles.
  • Shopping list with “I’ll get it”Whoever takes something taps it, so nobody buys it twice. In the shop the list sorts itself by aisle, even without signal.
  • “Where to buy?”On a product you set where you get it, say the chemist or the weekly market. The list shows it right next to the item. The app never fills this in by itself.
  • Discreet itemsOthers see private items only as a neutral placeholder, or not at all. Saturday’s present stays a surprise.
  • Ticked means put awayWhat you tick off in the shop goes straight into stock, if you have it set up that way.
03Treats

Do each other a good turn.

It is half past seven and Lea is putting Mila to bed. Tonight it takes a while. Between two pages of the bedtime story she taps “Something sweet”, then vanilla ice cream.

Till’s phone buzzes: “Lea asks you for something”. One tap on “I’ll bring it”. By the time she comes out of the kids’ room the ice cream is waiting, spoon included. The evening feels a little shorter after that.

That is what Treats is for. Whoever is stuck, with bedtime, ill in bed or after a long day, asks for a small something from what is in the house. Someone else brings it.

How a wish travels

  1. Lea asks for somethingSomething sweet, something to drink, a snack or a free-text wish. Plus a note like “with a spoon, please”.
  2. The message goes outIf Lea asked one person, only their phone buzzes. Otherwise everyone who can right now hears about it.
  3. “I’ll bring it” or “Can’t right now”One tap in the notification, with a short reason like “In a few minutes” if needed.

Then a small thank you. Once, only to the person who brought it. No points, no tally of who did what.

The same for visitors and parties

  • For youFor everyone who lives here. If you live alone, this part simply does not show up.
  • GuestsVisitors, the babysitter or grandparents get a link or QR code and ask for something from what is in the house. With their name, so it is clear whose coffee it is.
  • PartyThe TV shows a QR code to join and what is ready. The kitchen gets its own view, and the party ends by itself.

You decide what is on the menu: things from stock, or menu-only items like tap water or a cappuccino.

04Cooking

What are we eating tonight? Decided in a minute.

ToHuus suggests what fits what you have and plans the week with you. Anyone who wants to can vote.

  • What works right now?What you can cook now, what lacks just one thing and what needs using up. Or one tap on “You decide for us”.
  • Week planFree evenings fill up with fitting recipes, rotating over several weeks if you like. Whatever the week is missing goes on the list in one tap.
  • Vote togetherEveryone says no, yes or super on their own phone, or one phone goes round the table. No ranking, no debate at the stove.
  • Servings per eveningBolognese for six instead of four? The amount applies to that evening, to the shopping and to what comes out of stock after cooking. The recipe stays as it is.
05Recipes

Your version, not the internet’s.

Recipes come from wherever you already keep them. Then you cook them the way your household does.

  • From other appsAny recipe page with structured data, plus Mealie, Tandoor, Paprika, Nextcloud Cookbook, KitchenOwl, Grocy and CSV. With a vision model, photos and plain text too.
  • Units that fitCups, ounces and pounds become grams and millilitres if you cook metric. German and Swedish households get the ingredient lines translated, without any translation service.
  • With any AIA ready-made prompt to copy: put a photo or a cookbook page into the chat, paste the answer back into ToHuus.
  • Cookidoo via Home AssistantYour Cookidoo week comes in through Home Assistant. Thermomix steps keep time, temperature and speed.
  • The house versionRice instead of potatoes, no coriander, oat milk instead of milk. Set it once and it applies to suggestions, cooking and the shopping list. The original stays untouched.
  • Photos you can zoom intoOne tap opens a photo full screen, and then you can zoom in.
  • Who liked it?Everyone rates recipes and keeps favourites. Dislikes and allergies feed into suggestions, and a dish with an allergen is never planned automatically.
06Stock

Stock without a spreadsheet.

Nobody has to count pasta. Start with what runs out and leave the rest.

  • “Only what runs out”A start mode for anyone who does not want an inventory. Switch to everything later without losing anything.
  • Half full is an answerFruit and bread are full, half, low or empty. Packs get counted, some things not at all.
  • Labels for leftoversA box of leftovers is put away in two taps, with a date, a short code and a QR label. Scanning it opens eat, thaw, throw away or plan.
  • Where is what?Every item can have an exact spot. Type “plasters” and you know which shelf.
  • Scan a barcodeProduct name and photo come from Open Food Facts.
07Receipts & spending

The receipt puts things away.

Digital receipts and orders bring your stock up to date. Typing is the fallback, not the plan.

  • Receipts from many shopsGerman eBons and pickup notes, receipts from Lidl, Aldi, dm and others, plus UK, US and Swedish formats. As PDF, photo, QR code or through your own receipt inbox.
  • Learns from correctionsFix once what a receipt abbreviation stands for, and it applies to the next receipt from that shop. You can see the rules and delete them.
  • Check, don’t guessEvery import goes through a short review: substitutions, missing items, new products.
  • Spending without a ledgerTotals per week, month or year, by shop and category, straight from the receipts. Budgets and CSV export too.
08Flatmates, family, guests

For everyone who lives here. And for the ones just visiting.

Everyone has their own account and their face in the app. Guests only see what you share.

  • Invite by e-mail or linkWhoever joins gets a personal link, by e-mail or as a QR code. They sign in with a passkey, a password or an e-mail link. The link works once and for seven days.
  • Permanent guests with levelsThe aunt, the babysitter or a flatmate’s partner see as much as you want: treats only, stock too, recipes too, or the whole day to day. Settings and spending stay with you.
  • Guests with namesAnyone asking for something through a guest link types their name once. The phone remembers it.
  • Kids without a loginIn families a tablet becomes a kids’ screen: wish for something, look up where things are. Every wish waits for a parent.
  • Babysitter linkA link shows where things are, with your note on top. With Home Assistant, selected voice commands work too. Locks, alarms and cameras are never on the list.
09Away

Holidays without forgotten yoghurt.

Tell ToHuus when you are away. It plans the rest around the trip.

  • Use it up beforeIn the days before, suggestions use up what would otherwise go off. Nothing fresh goes on the list.
  • Packing listWhat comes along from stock is booked out, and back in after the trip.
  • Quiet while awayWhile you are gone, only urgent notifications come through and automatic shopping suggestions pause.
  • Coming homeThe welcome list is ready before you unlock the door, along with a look at what expired in the meantime.
10Smart home

Home Assistant, both ways.

If you already have automations, ToHuus joins in instead of replacing them.

  • Installed via HACSA dedicated integration with ready-made dashboards, MIT licensed.
  • Two ways, per topicShopping list as a to-do list for Assist, meal plan and home care as calendars, sensors for tonight and low stock. Per topic you decide whether ToHuus syncs with a list or calendar you already have in Home Assistant.
  • Kitchen displayAn old tablet or the TV shows the day, dark mode included. E-ink frames get a ready-made picture.
11Wherever you are

Telegram, Shortcuts, AI assistants.

Things land where you already type or talk.

AI assistants via MCP

Claude, ChatGPT and other assistants connect straight to your household. A photo of the receipt is enough.

Telegram and friends

Write “2 milk, bread” in the chat and it is on the list. Notifications also come by push, Gotify, Matrix, ntfy or e-mail.

12Privacy & self-hosting

It stays with you.

One Docker container, one SQLite file. No account with us, no tracking, nothing phones home. Everything that talks to the outside world is optional.

Runs almost anywhere
Docker on a NAS or a small VPS.
Your data
One SQLite file, JSON export, nightly backups, optionally to WebDAV or Nextcloud. A backup runs before every database migration.
Sign-in
Passkeys, password with a second factor, e-mail link, OpenID Connect and a PIN on the shared tablet.
Reading photos
Optional, with a model of your choice through an OpenAI-compatible API. Locally too, for example with Ollama.
Languages and regions
English, German and Swedish. The region sets currency, date format and which receipts to expect.
Light and dark
The household picks light, dark or like the device. If you like, everyone decides for themselves.
Licences
The app is licensed under the AGPL-3.0, the Home Assistant integration under MIT. No company behind it, no subscription.

Install

  1. Get it and set it upIn .env you fill in BASE_URL, your name and a password. Every other option is listed with comments in .env.example.
  2. Start itRun docker compose up -d --build, then open http://localhost:8080. Your data lives in ./data.
  3. Empty or demoOn first start you choose to begin empty or to look around with demo data first. One tap removes it later.
  4. Put HTTPS in frontYour usual reverse proxy is enough. Passkeys and the share menu need HTTPS. Then add the app to your home screen.
Terminal
git clone https://github.com/tillheidrich/tohuus-hub.git
cd tohuus-hub
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d --build
.env
BASE_URL=https://home.example.org
ADMIN_NAME=Alex
ADMIN_PASSWORD=change-me
LANGUAGE=en # en, de or sv
TZ=Europe/Berlin

To HuusLow German for “at home”. Spoken along the North Sea and the Baltic.

ToHuus started in an ordinary family routine. One person knew where everything was and what was running out, the other one asked. The one rule while building it: it has to work on a tired Tuesday.

It is built for every kind of home, the studio flat without a freezer just as much as the flat share where everyone has their own shelf in the fridge.

It is free and open source. Issues and pull requests are welcome, and so are translations.
